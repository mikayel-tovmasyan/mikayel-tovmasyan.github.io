Research

My research lies in public economics and political economy, with a particular focus on offshore finance and the economic consequences of geopolitical policies and political transitions. I examine how financial sanctions and electoral turnovers affect offshore banking, alongside broader questions in public economics about educational policy, the effects of housing regulation on crime, and the voluntary provision of biodiversity information as a public resource. My work combines large-scale banking and administrative data, leaked offshore records, and citizen-generated observations with empirical approaches to causal inference, including regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences, event studies, and synthetic control.

Bypassing Sanctions:
Hide 'N Seek in Tax Havens

with Dominika Langenmayr and Sebastian Vosseler

Revise and resubmit, Journal of International Economics

Do financial sanctions induce targets to move assets beyond the sanctioning country's reach?

Read paper PDF · Google Drive↗ (opens in a new tab)
Read full abstract

Do financial sanctions induce targets to move assets beyond the sanctioning country's reach? We combine quarterly BIS data on bilateral bank deposits with U.S. and EU financial sanctions from 1996–2023 and exploit variation in sanction timing across countries. Individual sanctions increase deposits in tax havens: by about 18% after U.S. sanctions and 46% after EU sanctions. Deposits simultaneously fall in sanctioning jurisdictions, especially after EU sanctions. When sanctions are more severe because the U.S. and EU sanction jointly, haven deposits do not increase on average; deposits in other foreign countries fall and domestic deposits rise. Major sanctions likewise restrict foreign deposits more broadly. Combining leaked offshore data with individual-level sanctions, we find that sanctioned individuals rely on existing shell companies, while other residents establish new offshore structures, potentially anticipating further sanctions.

Other working papers

Elections and Offshore Deposits: Evidence from Close Electoral Turnovers

with Sebastian Vosseler

We examine whether electoral turnovers affect offshore bank deposits, considering competing channels involving accountability, enforcement expectations and political uncertainty. The analysis combines quarterly bilateral BIS foreign bank deposit & offshore leaks data, with regression discontinuity and difference-in-differences designs.

The Education Gambit: Chess, Cognitive Skills, and a Natural Experiment in Armenia

I investigate whether Armenia's compulsory primary-school chess program affects students' cognitive performance in the school and long-term participation in competitive chess. The analysis combines adjacent-grade difference-in-differences & triple-differences designs using mathematics and language contest data with cohort comparisons and synthetic-control analysis of competitive-chess records.

Work in progress

Film Publicity and the Allocation of Biodiversity Contributions

I study whether film publicity changes the allocation of voluntary contributions to biodiversity information—a public resource whose provision depends on private decisions. Using weekly counts of crowdsourced, species-specific wildlife observations from iNaturalist and a seasonal difference-in-differences design, I compare records concerning rabbits and hares with those concerning a fixed group of other animals around the 2018 U.S. release of Peter Rabbit.

Does Rent Control Increase Crime? Evidence from Germany

with Seraphin Schwarzbauer

We investigate whether housing-market regulation has broader social consequences beyond rents, focusing on the effects of Germany's rent regulation for new tenancies (Mietpreisbremse) on crime. Using municipality-level police-recorded crime data, we compare designated and non-designated areas before and after the reform, examining outcomes such as burglary, assault, and domestic violence, as well as differences by pre-reform rental exposure and housing conditions.
